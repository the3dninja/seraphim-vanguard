# Seraphim Vanguard

A retro NES-style vertical space shooter for phone browsers. Built with Phaser 3, TypeScript and Vite.

**Play:** https://the3dninja.github.io/seraphim-vanguard/

## How to play

- **Drag anywhere** to move the ship. It follows your finger's movement, so your thumb never covers it.
- Your ship **fires on its own**.
- Shoot enemies to drop **credits** (bronze, silver and gold bars), then spend them at the **vendor** station halfway through each level (from level 2 on).
- Tap an **item button** (bottom right, one per item slot) to use that item.
- On a computer: arrow keys or WASD to move, Space to use your first item, 1/2/3 for a specific item slot.

Progress saves automatically in your browser each time you reach Base, and whenever you change your ship or buy an upgrade there.

Playtest helpers (add to the end of the link, combine with `&`):

- `?god`: your ship can't be destroyed.
- `?cheats` (also on with `?test` or `?edit`, and always when running locally): a CHEAT MENU button at the bottom of the pause menu. It has God Mode, Demigod Mode (hits hurt, but the ship comes straight back with full health when destroyed), Infinite Items, Game Speed (1x, 2x or 4x), Skip To Location (reloads the level 0 to 1 of the way through its waves; 1 is right before the boss), and Unlock All Levels. Cheats last until the page is reloaded. With the Tuner on, its button moves into the pause menu during missions.
- `?rich`: tops your credits up to 5000 when you reach Base, for trying upgrades.
- `?level=3`: sets your next mission to level 3 (any built level works) when you reach Base.
- `?gateend`: on level 14, Maw Gate, skips straight to the last gate (use with `?level=14`).
- `?test`: combat test mode. Two buttons at the bottom pause the game: the crossed swords switch looping enemy wave sequences on or off (they stack), and the ship puts any weapon or gear in slot 1 or 2 with its level (1 to 5) and any item in slot 1 to 3 (items never run out but have a cooldown). The ship respawns with full health when destroyed, and nothing is saved or added to the Bestiary.
- `?tune` (also on with `?test` or `?edit`): a TUNER button at the top opens the Tuner, which pauses the game. It has a card for every weapon, gear, item and foe (bosses, the Hive's barrier cells and the treasure hauler included), each with its name, its in-game description and its numbers. Numbers that grow with level have their value at level 1 and how much it changes each level (with the result at levels 1 to 5 shown above); some, like shots per volley, have a box for each of levels 1 to 5. Foes have their health (decimals are fine), drops and how much of each damage type they take, in % (100 normal, 200 double, 50 half, 0 immune). Each number can be changed with - and + or typed in. APPLY puts edits into the game at once; CLEAR puts everything back to the game's numbers. EXPORT lists only the values that differ from the game, to copy and paste back so they can be written into the code. HISTORY keeps exports not added yet, and lists the ones already added to the game, each with a button to go back to the numbers from before it. Tuned values are kept in this browser and only used with `?tune`, `?test` or `?edit`.
- `?edit` (also a LEVEL EDITOR button on the title screen with `?test`): the Level Editor, over the title screen. LOAD LEVEL lists every story level and side mission; NEW LEVEL starts a blank one. Tabs along the top are the whole level (`0. LEVEL`) and each segment; under a segment, WAVES, HAZARDS and SEGMENT (its name, theme, mini boss and radio call). Each shows its part of the level as text, in the same form as `src/data/levels.ts`, and APPLY reads it back (a mistake is pointed out by line). WAVES also lists the waves: tapping one opens the wave editor (kind, pattern, count, position, delay, wait for clear, and an option to take a delay change off the next wave so later waves stay put), with its own UNDO, COPY, PASTE, CODE (to that wave's line) and APPLY. UNDO steps back through applies. PLAY flies the level from the segment (or wave) on screen with the combat test loadout: no vendor stops, the ship respawns, nothing is saved, and the pause menu's RETURN TO EDITOR goes back to the same place. EXPORT gives the level as text to paste back so it can be written into the code. Drafts are kept in this browser. Also turns on the Cheat Menu and the Tuner.

For example `https://the3dninja.github.io/seraphim-vanguard/?god&rich&level=3`.

## What's in this version

- Title screen with Continue / New Game
- **Fits any screen**: the play area is always the same 180 x 320, so every phone plays the same. The space around it is solid black; on tall phones the hearts and credits move into the strip above it and the weapon, gear and item buttons into the strip below (kept clear of the notch and home bar), and on shorter or wider screens they stay over the play area. It refits when the phone turns
- **Pause menu**: the pause button at the top center (between the hearts, now eight to a row, and the credits), Escape or P, or leaving the game for another app pauses it. RESUME, ABORT MISSION (back to Base as if the ship were destroyed: keeps 25% of the credits picked up), OPTIONS (nothing yet) and QUIT (closes the game where the browser allows it, otherwise back to the title screen); Abort and Quit ask first
- Level 1, Outpost Run (the tutorial): Darts and Weavers in two legs that flow straight into each other (no vendor yet: the first stop is on level 2), and a final wave
- Level 2, Scrapyard Belt: Scrap Drone swarms, Gunboats that park and fire 3-shot spreads, and drifting wrecks that block shots from both sides
- Level 3, Talus Asteroids: rocks that split when shot, Bombers that lay mines, and the **Rockjaw** mini boss. Break off its three armor plates to expose the core; it throws boulders, and fires spreads once its core is bare. Now and then it switches from swaying to a diving figure eight, and it moves 25% faster once the core is bare. Its health bar is the core; a thinner blue bar under it shows the armor (the Sporeheart's petals show the same way). It drops 175 credits
- Level 4, Ice Ring of Halva: falling ice shards, ice sheets that send the ship sliding, Drillers that line up and ram from the sides, and Vesparan Lancers that blink a warning line before firing a beam down it
- Level 5, Vesparan Blockade: Bulwarks behind shields that soak up shots until they break, capital ship guns that lean in from the screen edges, and **Admiral Kade's Dreadnought**. Destroy its four turrets; then its bridge sweeps two lasers in from the sides (2 damage a hit) and drops its shield while they fire, so hit it from the lane under it. It drops 450 credits, and when it blows Kade's escape pod arcs away off the top of the screen
- Level 6, Solar Forge: **Vesparan Carriers** park and launch four Darts each, and solar flares sweep down the screen. A shelter rock drifts in before each flare; sit in the dark lane under it
- Level 7, Ember Nebula: **Mine Layers** seed proximity mines that burst when you get close, and lightning strikes down a column after it flashes
- Level 8, Silent Relay: **Phantoms** cloak (shots pass through them) and decloak to fire, laser fences blink on and off as they scroll down (cross while off), and **The Watcher** mini boss. It cloaks, then decloaks beside decoy afterimages; only the real one takes damage (decoys look a shade fainter and flicker). Below two thirds health it brings three decoys that fire too, and seeds mines. Below a third it starts the Hall of Mirrors: four copies sway in a row and swap places, taking turns to lock a beam onto the ship, while the real one fires spreads. It drops 175 credits
- Level 9, Blight Colony: the Hollow appears. **Spore Pods** burst into Seekers when destroyed, mixed in with Vesparan ships, and spore clouds drift down the screen
- Level 10, Throne of Vespar: **Tendrils** join in, blast doors slide across the screen, and **Emperor Vespar, Possessed**. His flagship hides behind a shield that ion breaks fast; when the hull gives out, the Hollow parasite tears free, lunges at the ship and lays Spore Pods. Plasma burns it. It drops 450 credits
- Level 11, The Rift: starts in normal space with Rook's raiders flying as wingmen, against **Corrupted** Raiders mixed in with the Hollow. Where the vendor would arrive, the station stops above the ship, the rift tears open and pulls both in. Inside, space rushes by three times as fast, the Hollow comes more often (with a few Corrupted ships) and the gravity wells drift twice as fast. Then a second, dimmer tear opens ahead and keeps opening past the screen's edges, letting the ship out into Hollow space under a weird purple star and a purple-green nebula, where the vendor waits. The final leg opens with spores and Corrupted Raiders, ramps up the Hollow, and ends on spores and Corrupted Scrap Drones. **Broodlings** swarm, **Husks** split into Seekers, and gravity wells pull the ship and bend shots
- **The Corrupted** (level 11 on): Raider, Drone Swarm and Vesparan ships overgrown by the Hollow, with four times the health, more shots per attack, new attack patterns and their own takes on each formation. Corrupted Darts meander and fire, Weavers fire fans, Gunboats alternate fans and rings, Bombers drop three bombs, Trappers' net mines throw shots, Drones take potshots, Drillers spray shots and make a second run, Mine Layers seed six mines that burst wider, Lancers fire three beams, Bulwarks fire fans behind a tougher shield, Carriers launch Corrupted Darts, and Phantoms fire fans each time they decloak. The big ones spill Seekers when shot down. Raider and Vesparan waves no longer appear after the Vespar fight
- Level 12, Hive Reef: coral walls cut across the screen (shots carve through them), and **Mimics** copy the ship's look
- Level 13, Black Bloom: the screen is dark except a pool of light around the ship and glowing enemies, and the **Brood Queen** mini boss. She lays pods, fires fans, flickers and charges, and heals if you stop hitting her. Plasma hurts her most. It drops 175 credits
- Level 14, Maw Gate: the Vesparan fleet fights in the background, three wingmen fly with you, and tentacles sweep in from the sides, more often as each segment goes on. It opens in plain space, then flies in through the gate's hull: a narrow mouth ringed with runes, and a passage of girders jutting from the walls and pillars up the middle (they hurt the ship and stop its shots, but let enemy shots through). Then it opens out. Waves come closer together and grow through both segments. Hollow enemies warp in five at a time inside **warp rings**: huge rings, wider than the screen, that drift down with a 100 px gap top and bottom to fly through (the ring itself hurts and stops the ship's shots); once the ship is inside, near the bottom, the warp circles form. A ring comes a third of the way in and just before the vendor, then after the vendor every 7 to 12 s after the last has passed, closing to 3 to 6 s, until two thirds of the way. After the last wave comes **the last gate**: a grander ring with glowing runes, rings behind it and a green and purple gas giant with raging storms, all slowing to a stop lined up together. A word from Vespar and Reyes, an ambient red alarm, then three escalating waves of warp-ins. The last gate is an **arena**: nothing scrolls, so enemies there keep their side-to-side moves and dashes but hold their height and stay until killed (a Tendril that lunges off the screen comes back in from the top), and credits don't fall. Once they're beaten the rings spin up, light gathers in the middle, a vortex opens and pulls the ship in, and the screen goes white, straight back to Base with no level clear text
- Level 15, Heart of the Hollow: the longest run, with no vendor, in five legs that flow straight into each other (nothing is cleared and the scenery fades from one to the next). Arteries, tentacles, coral and gravity wells first; then a **living tunnel** that narrows, winds back and forth and opens into chambers (its walls hurt and stop shots); then a **spore haze** and **The Sporeheart** mini boss (shoot off its petals, hit the core while it's open); then **walls of growth** to shoot through, with **eggs** that hatch into **Larvae** if not burned in time, and **The Brood Wyrm** mini boss (only the head can kill it; it lays eggs and dives at you); then **The Hollow Mind**. Shoot its six eyes, break its membrane, burn its core, then it splits open into a maw that makes black holes (they pull the ship and eat shots) and warps space. Between phases it retreats and calls its brood. It ends the campaign
- **Side missions**: ten optional levels on small moons beside levels 2, 4, 7, 8, 9, 12 and 14 (one beside 2, 7, 9 and 14, two beside the others), for extra credits before the boss levels. Each unlocks when you beat its level and stays open until you leave that system of five levels; flying a system's last level with side missions still open asks first. Pick one on the star map and Launch; you return to Base, and the map still shows your ship at the next level. After every mission you land on the star map; a SIDE MISSION UNLOCKED banner flashes near newly unlocked moons, and the first one is picked so its briefing shows. Storm Eye's moon has a stack of three gold bars in its corner until it's cleared. They mix enemies you've met in new ways, run from shorter than level 2 to just longer than level 6 (the description says which), and add their own hazards: scrap presses that slam shut, crosswinds, falling embers, blackouts and sweeping searchlights (hide behind satellite debris). Storm Eye's storm builds over the run, from lightning alone to embers, then a long peak with hot wind and two ember showers, and dies down over the last waves, leaving a little red in the sky. It ends with **treasure**: after a short calm a derelict cargo hauler drifts in; break it open for 225 credits before it gets three quarters of the way down, or it breaks up on its own and the credits are gathered in. Glacier Vault's ice builds as it goes, to twice the ice sheets and three times the shards by the end, and keeps coming through the boss fight. Three end in a mini boss in new colors: Frostjaw (half again Rockjaw's health and fire rate after its 1.8x boost; it sheds ice shards while armored and fires them in fans once its core is bare), the Hollow Watcher and the Brood Matriarch
- Levels 6 to 8 run about 50% longer than levels 1 to 5. Level 11's alien segment is a bit shorter than level 6's first segment and its final leg is 50% longer; levels 12 to 14 use that longer length for both segments, and level 15 is longer than two of them
- **Radio calls** come in a comms box at the top of the screen: the speaker's 16x16 portrait, their name in their color, and the line wrapped beside it (18 letters a line, so line breaks in the level data are ignored). Kade and Vespar show Hollow-touched portraits until Vespar is freed after level 10, and Kade's dying call jitters. Reyes has a stand-in silhouette for now. The portraits are PNGs in `src/portraits/`, so a new drawing can replace one directly
- **Wingmen** (levels 11 to 14) each have their own gun: Rook fires bursts of three lasers, the Vesparan wingman fires ion bolts that stun and jump to two more enemies, and Admiral Kade (from Maw Gate on, flying point) lobs homing plasma that splashes. They stay with you through vendor stops (rolling down to the station with you) and fly off with you at the end
- **Credits**: at the end of a level, loose credits curve in toward the ship, turning harder the closer they get; a boss's gold bursts out as before and curves in too. The Credit Booster is rare early on (the Base vendor stocks it from level 4)
- **Damage types**: weapons do kinetic, laser, plasma, ion or explosive damage. Enemies and bosses take double from their weakness and half from what they resist (Target Scanner shows weaknesses as dots)
- **Weapons**: Pulse, Spread, Twin, Homing, Plasma, Ion (breaks shields, stuns), Wave, Rail (pierces), Flak (bursts; from level 5 a direct hit sets off the burst), Sidewinder (small homing missiles that seek out to each side), Bio Seeker (spores latch on and eat away at what they hit; more can cling to one enemy each level) and Mine Layer (explosive: drops a mine behind the ship every 1.5 s, faster each level; each lasts 5 s and blasts everything within 25 px when an enemy touches it or it runs out, and from level 2 it goes off when an enemy just comes near). Weapon and gear descriptions show their numbers at the level you have, and name damage types in their colors. Homing missiles explode, wider each level. Ion arcs reach further each level and stun from level 3. Hits look heavier as a weapon levels up: more sparks in its damage color, and from level 2 a flash ring that grows each level
- **Items**: Repair, Shield, Overdrive, Cluster Bomb, EMP, Decoy Flare, Credit Booster, Heal Drone, Full Repair, Nova, Time Warp and Rewind Beacon (restarts from the last checkpoint: the level start, the last vendor, or the start of a level 15 leg)
- **Active gear** has its own buttons above the item bar (Q and E on a keyboard): Dodge, Blink, Afterburner (rams small ships; from level 5 big ones too, never bosses; while it burns, what it can ram glows teal and the rest orange), Barrel Roll (reflects shots) and Burst. **Rear Guard** makes the weapon in the same slot fire backward too. **Augments** change your shots: Incendiary Ammo (glowing rounds that scorch and set enemies burning; a burning enemy carries a pulsing flame until the burn ends, and one that burns to death goes up in a burst of flames), Ion Coating, Shaped Charges, Overcharger and Target Scanner. Anti-Missile shoots down shots near the ship, those level with it and ahead first (two at a time at level 5)
- **Upgrade kits** (Weapon Kit, Gear Kit) from vendors raise a weapon or gear a level; apply one on the spot or keep it and use it from the vendor's Sell tab or the Ship screen
- **Gear** (from level 6): Hull Plating (+2 max health) and Credit Magnet at the Base vendor; Deflector (absorbs a hit, recharges) and Satellite (an orbiting drone that fires) as in-level vendor exclusives. Gear slots 1 and 2 and gear levels 1 to 5 are in the Base vendor's Upgrade tab
- Item stacks show against their cap, like 1/3, in flight, on the vendor's slot strip and on the Ship screen
- The vendor lists 3 entries a page, each with a colored line saying what kind it is (Weapon, Gear, Augment, Upgrade, Item, Rare Item). The page arrows, the info box and Storage sit at fixed spots below the list, in that order. In-level vendors (the station scrolls in under the ship as it rolls and sinks into it) have Buy, Sell and **Repair** tabs; Repair fixes hearts for a price per heart. In-level stock leans toward stronger gear as the levels go on, with a small chance of something strong early
- Base menu: your ship sits in the middle (tap it for the Ship screen), with picture tiles in the corners for Shop (the vendor), Map, Foes (the Bestiary) and Options (not built yet), and a Launch bar below
- **Bestiary**: every enemy and boss, one faction per page in story order (Raiders, Drone Swarm, Talus Beasts, Vesparan Empire, The Hollow over two pages, The Corrupted); swipe left or right, or use the arrows, to turn pages. Pictures fill the top half at their in-game size (only bosses too big for a tile are shrunk); tap one to read its name, weaknesses and resistances, a short description, how many you've destroyed, and whether it's a boss. Enemies you haven't faced show as ? and read UNDISCOVERED, and a faction stays ??? until you've faced one of its members. Kills and sightings are saved, and kept even when the ship is lost
- Base vendor **Upgrade** tab: Hull plates (+1 max health, up to 10), a second weapon slot (both weapons fire), item slots 2 and 3, and weapon levels 1 to 5 (more damage each level; faster fire at level 3; twin shots or 5-way spread at level 5)
- Star map: all 15 levels on a route, with side mission moons beside them. The next level is picked, and you can tap the one after it (or any cleared level, or a side mission) to read about it, then Launch the next level or an open side mission. Common enemies show, unlabeled under the description, as icons with 1 to 3 pips for how many there are; enemies you haven't met show as ?
- Ship screen: drag weapons, gear and items from Base Storage onto the ship's slots (or tap one, then tap a slot; arrow keys and Enter work too). Locked slots show their price
- Placeholder pixel art drawn in code (`src/art.ts`), placeholder prices (`src/data/items.ts`, `src/inventory.ts`)

The full design is in the "Space Shooter Design Spec" doc in the Simple Games project.

## Working on it

You need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) 20 or newer.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev      # local server; open the printed address on your phone (same Wi-Fi) or computer
npm run build    # production build into dist/
```

Every push to `main` rebuilds the game and publishes it to the play link above (see `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`).
